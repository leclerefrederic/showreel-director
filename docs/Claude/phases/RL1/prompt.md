# Phase RL1: the rail gets two groups and Clap becomes one of its entries

Read CLAUDE.md, then docs/Claude/ux-notes.md (the entries "Visual direction", "Motion" and "Clap in the rail (owner, after DS1)"), docs/Claude/phases/DS1/report.md, and the code: the view rail and the shell built in DS1 (ContentView.swift and the rail views), the right panel and its two tabs (Inspector, Ask Clap), the sliding panes of Look and Project (ProjectSettingsPanel.swift and what presents them), ChatView and ChatModel (state of the agent, running, the proposals mechanism and its banner), the centre pane's model, Theme.swift and the `IconRole` table, `StudioSettings` and `.studio/ui.json`, `disabledWithReason` and Availability. Follow every rule of CLAUDE.md (never run git, English interface, skill untouched, no out-of-scope work: an idea goes in docs/Claude/ideas.md in one line). The owner waived the open questions of docs/Claude/ideas.md for the redesign: the defaults that exist today stand. Views stay free of business logic: the rail's rules live in ShowreelKit with tests.

## Goal

Ask Clap becomes an entry of the left rail, next to Project and Build. The right side shows exactly one panel at a time, chosen from the rail. A centre view and a right panel are independent. Clap's icon shows what Clap is doing.

## Step 0 (read-only, 10 lines at the top of docs/Claude/phases/RL1/report.md)

a) How the right panel is composed today (the Inspector and Ask Clap tabs, the Look and Project sliding panes: what slides, what is replaced, how each is presented and dismissed, the spring used).
b) What the rail does today for Film, Look, Build and Project, and the exclusivity noted in DS1.
c) How the agent's state is exposed (running, waiting for the owner, the proposals count) and where the proposals banner is drawn.
d) The keyboard shortcuts already in use (list them) and the focus handling of the Clap input.
e) What `ui.json` already remembers.

## What to build

1. **Two groups in the rail, separated by a thin line.** Centre views: Film, Build. Right panels: Inspector, Look, Project, Clap. Same icon-and-label look as DS1, the selected entry on the selected surface with the accent; icons come from the `IconRole` table (add roles for the new entries, with a test that every role is mapped and distinct where they must be).
1b. **Shell geometry (owner's addition).** Under the top bar, which stays exactly as it is today: the rail, the left panels (Storyboard on top, Resources below) and the right panel each take the **full height** of the window. The **centre column** holds the preview on top and, under it, the strip (the timeline), so the strip is only as wide as the centre column, no longer the whole window. Keep the 6 pt gaps and the rounded panels of DS1. Because the strip is narrower, check that "Fit" (the whole film in the width), the zoom memory, the ruler labels, the grid and the marks still work and read well at the centre column's width, and set sensible minimum widths for the regions (a pure `ShellLayout` in ShowreelKit: minimum widths, the minimum window width they imply, and which regions span the full height, with tests); the window cannot be resized below that minimum, and the reset control of the view group (see 1c) restores the standard proportions. Vocabulary: the strip is titled **Film** (it is the timeline of the film, under its preview) and the left list stays **Storyboard** (the plan of the scenes and transitions). Change the displayed text only (the strip's header "Storyboard · 15 scenes · 1:30.0 of 90 s" becomes "Film · 15 scenes · 1:30.0 of 90 s", its tooltips, menus and the legend where the strip is named); keep the stored keys, gate names and the user-facing words "Validate storyboard" and "Go" as they are unless they name the strip.
1c. **Film placement toggle (owner's addition).** The owner chooses where the Film strip lives, with ONE button placed in a small **view group at the top right of the top bar**. **Its icon is a pictogram of the layout currently chosen** (the owner's rule: the icon represents the arrangement in use): a small drawing of the window with its regions, the Film strip drawn under the centre column in one state and along the whole bottom in the other. A click switches to the other arrangement; the pictogram changes with the spring, or at once under "Reduce motion". The tooltip names the state and the action ("Film under the preview. Click for full width"), and the accessibility label and value say the same. Draw the two pictograms as small SwiftUI shapes from the theme tokens (SF Symbols has no such glyph), and register them as icon roles in the `IconRole` table so that a later skin can replace them. The two states are: (a) **Under the preview** (the default described in 1b: the strip is as wide as the centre column and the rail, the left panels and the right panel keep the full height); (b) **Full width**: the strip becomes a block along the bottom that spans from the left panel to the right panel (under the left panels, the centre column and the right panel; the rail stays full height), so the left panels, the preview and the right panel are shorter. Everything else in the top bar stays exactly as it is today; the new view group sits at its right end (next to the Checks badge, say in the report where exactly). The choice is a project interface setting remembered per project in `.studio/ui.json` (the default is "Under the preview"), and changing it animates the move of the panels with the same spring as the panes (a plain change under "Reduce motion"). The strip's behaviour is the same in both modes (Fit, zoom, ruler, grid, marks, drag and drop); only its width and the regions' heights differ. `ShellLayout` describes both modes (frames of every region for a given window size, minimum widths and heights for each mode, the minimum window size each implies) so that both are tested without a screen. Do NOT add show or hide controls for the side panels: that idea goes in docs/Claude/ideas.md in one line.
1d. **User sizes are independent of the layout, and a reset icon (owner's rules).** The widths and heights the owner chooses by dragging (the left panels' width, the right panel's width, the Film strip's height, the divider between Storyboard and Resources, the share of the preview) are stored as their own values in `.studio/ui.json`, one set for the project, **never changed by switching the Film placement**: the placement only decides how the regions are arranged around those values (the frames are derived from them). When the window is too small for the stored values, clamp them when drawing and never overwrite what was stored (making the window bigger brings the choices back). Add a **reset icon** in the same view group at the top right, next to the layout button: it puts the sizes back to the **standard proportions** (a constant in `ShellLayout`) and does **not** change the chosen placement. It replaces the existing "Reset layout" control of the top bar if that control does the same thing (say in the report what the old control did and that it was replaced, so there is only one reset). The reset has a tooltip ("Restore the standard proportions"), an accessibility label, a visible reaction (the panels move to their standard sizes with the spring, at once under "Reduce motion") and, when the sizes are already standard, it is disabled with the reason ("The proportions are already standard") through `disabledWithReason`. The reset is one undoable step if the app has a place for interface undo; if not, say so and do not invent one. The reset icon is an `IconRole` too.
2. **One right panel at a time, independent of the centre.** Clicking a right-panel entry opens that panel; clicking the active one closes the right side; the Inspector is what shows when the owner selects a scene or a transition and no other right panel is open (say in the report how you resolved the case where an Inspector selection happens while Clap or Look is open: keep the open panel and show a small mark on the Inspector entry, unless step 0 shows a better existing rule). Choosing Film or Build never closes a right panel, and opening a right panel never changes the centre. This removes the exclusivity DS1 left.
3. **Clap's panel.** Remove the right panel's top tabs (Inspector, Ask Clap). Clicking Clap opens the sliding pane with the agent: the conversation, with Memory, Sessions and Journal as tabs inside it, exactly as the Ask Clap tab holds them today. The pane slides in from the right with the same spring and slight bounce that Look and Project use (reuse it, no new motion tokens in this phase).
4. **Clap's entry shows its state.** A pulse on the icon while the agent is working; a badge with the number of proposals waiting for the owner (the existing proposals mechanism and its banner stay as they are; a click on the badge opens the Clap panel at the proposals). With "Reduce motion" on, the pulse becomes a static dot. The same state is in the entry's accessibility label and tooltip ("Clap, working", "Clap, 2 proposals waiting").
5. **A shortcut.** One shortcut that opens Clap and puts the cursor in its input from anywhere in the project window. Choose one that is not already used (list the used ones in the report) and show it in the tooltip.
6. **Remembered state.** Which right panel was open is remembered per project in `.studio/ui.json` and restored at opening (Clap included); a project with no choice opens with the Inspector.
7. **Action/reaction and availability.** Every entry that is unavailable says why and how to get it back (`disabledWithReason`), as the DS1 rail does. Keyboard: Tab reaches the rail and the arrows move between entries; each entry has an accessibility label that includes its state.
8. **Theme.** Colours, radii and type only from the tokens; no new literal.
9. **Nothing else changes.** No change to what the Inspector, Look, Project or Clap contain, to the Build, to the skill or to the shared tools. Clap as a drawer next to the Inspector (both visible) is out of scope: one line in docs/Claude/ideas.md.
