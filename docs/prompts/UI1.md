# Phase UI1: the left panel becomes the Storyboard (flat) with the Resources browser below

Read CLAUDE.md, then docs/ux-notes.md (section "Library, Scenes tab", the entry "Proposed restructure (owner, 2026-11-09 ...)" and its bullets), docs/studio-spec.md only for the library, the inspector and the drag and drop sections, and the code: LibraryPane and LibraryTab, the Scenes tab views (WorkspaceViews.swift), TransitionList (T2), SceneBadges, BuildIndicator, the Resources views (ResourceViews.swift), ChecksPanel, the inspector (InspectorViews.swift), DragDropViews.swift (DragRow, DropPlan, DragRun), ProjectModel selection and Availability.swift. Follow every rule of CLAUDE.md (never run git, English interface, skill untouched, no out-of-scope work: an idea goes in docs/ideas.md in one line). The views stay free of business logic: rules and models live in ShowreelKit with tests. UI principle: every action has a visible reaction, nothing is disabled silently (disabledWithReason, Availability).

## Vocabulary (use it in the interface and in the code comments)

Storyboard = the plan of the montage: scenes in order, their durations and their transitions. Film = the assembled, playable result. The strip at the bottom and the left list are two views of the same storyboard, with the selection kept in step.

## What to build

1. **Left panel without tabs for Resources and Scenes.** Split it vertically: the Storyboard list on top (about two thirds), the Resources browser below (about one third). A draggable divider, its position remembered per project (in `.studio/ui.json`, like the other interface settings). The Resources section can be collapsed to its header and can be opened to the full height of the panel ("Expand"), then restored; the state is remembered. The Transitions tab disappears (its content is merged, see 2). The Checks tab is replaced (see 5).
2. **The Storyboard list is flat.** One row per scene of the film, in order, no tree and no unfolding. Between two consecutive scenes, one thin line for the transition, in one sentence ("to Cross dissolve, 0.8 s", "to Cut"), keeping the marks T2 had ("approximate render at assembly", "instruction pending"). A click on a scene selects it everywhere (list, strip, inspector); a click on a transition line selects the marker on the strip and opens its inspector, as the diamond does. Reordering by the handle moves a scene and its outgoing transition together, with the gap that opens, as today. Under the list, a section "Not in the storyboard" for scenes that are archived or switched off, with the same reasons and the same way back that exist today (the eye, restore, and so on). Keep every action the Scenes tab has now (New, Draft, Lock all, rename in place, duplicate, archive, add scene) in a compact toolbar above the list.
3. **State icons only on each scene row, nothing else of detail.** After the handle: ID and name, then a small fixed set of icons in a fixed order: review state (to review, approved, to modify), locked, build state of the scene file (not generated, up to date, stale, edited by hand, failed test), layout warnings. At most four visible; each has a tooltip that gives the reason and, when it is unavailable or needs attention, the way to fix it. No icon without a tooltip. The icon rules (which icon for which state, the order, the tooltip text) are computed in ShowreelKit (a pure function with tests); the view draws them.
4. **The scene's tree moves to the Inspector as an overview.** The scene's content that the tree showed (composition elements in order, Effects, Look with what is inherited or replaced) becomes a read-only overview at the top of the Inspector's scene view, where a click on a node opens the matching Inspector tab on that element, as the tree did. Nothing that the tree offered may be lost: if a tree action has no place in the overview, say so in the report instead of dropping it silently.
5. **Checks.** Replace the Checks tab by a badge in the top bar with the count of red and orange items (the count the tab shows today). A click opens the list of checks in the centre pane, with the same grouping (Project, Resources, Scenes, Storyboard, Music, Build) and the same click-through to the element concerned. If nothing needs attention the badge says so. Add this to the report as a decision the owner can reverse (the alternative is a thin status row at the bottom of the left panel).
6. **Drop targets.** A resource dragged from the Resources section must be accepted by a scene row of the Storyboard list, by a frame of the strip, and by the scene's Inspector overview (the inspector had no scene target). The plain-text token `studio:resource:<id>` and `DragRun` stay as they are. A locked scene refuses with its reason; during a Build everything is refused with its reason. Remove the spring-loaded tab idea from the notes since the two sections are now visible together.
7. **Selection in step.** Selecting a scene or a transition in the list, on the strip or in the inspector selects it in the other two places. Keep the existing selection model; do not add a second one.
8. **Keyboard.** Arrow keys move between scenes in the list as the strip's arrows do; Delete switches a scene off (existing behaviour, with Undo). No new shortcut.

## Tests

Pure rules in ShowreelKit with unit tests: the flat list model (scenes in order, transition lines between pairs, the "Not in the storyboard" section, a single scene with no transition line); reordering moves a scene with its outgoing transition; the icon function (every state maps to one icon, fixed order, at most four, every icon has a tooltip with a reason); the selection stays in step between list, strip and inspector; drop acceptance for a scene row, a frame and the inspector overview, including the locked scene and the Build-in-progress refusals with their reasons; the layout memory (divider position, collapsed and expanded states) is saved and restored; the Checks badge count equals the sum the tab showed. Update the tests that referenced the Scenes and Transitions tabs or the Checks tab, and say which ones changed.
