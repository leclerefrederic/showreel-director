# Motion, the interface answers

The owner likes the motion that DS1 brought and wants more: an interface that responds and lets you understand what is happening. Motion is the visible half of the action/reaction principle, so it must explain cause and effect, not decorate.
- **One motion language, as tokens.** Durations (fast about 120 ms, base about 200 ms, slow about 300 ms) and easing curves (a decelerating curve for things that arrive, an accelerating one for things that leave, a small overshoot only for confirmations) defined once beside the colour tokens, with a speed scale a skin can set (including "off").
- **Patterns by meaning.** Selection moves (a highlight that slides, not jumps); a change of state shows itself (a status icon changes with a short morph or pulse, a switch slides); arrival and departure keep their place (a new scene opens a gap, a deleted one closes it, Undo plays it back); a refusal nudges the control and shows the reason where the eye is; progress is shown where the work is (the scene rows of a Build show waiting, writing, testing and repairing differently, a finished scene settles); a panel that opens or collapses moves from where it will live.
- **Rules.** Never block input while something animates; no motion longer than about 300 ms for a direct action; respect "Reduce motion" in the system settings (replace movement by a fade or nothing, keep the information); motion is never the only carrier of meaning (the icon, the text and the tooltip stay).
- **Where it goes.** Define the tokens and a small set of reusable modifiers at the start of DS2, then apply them block by block with the restyle (the strip and the lists in DS2, the inspector and the forms in DS3), each with a visible reaction in the hand-test list.
- **Reference the owner named.** Clicking Look in the rail opens a pane that slides in from the right with a slight bounce (a spring with a small overshoot). Keep that feel as the pattern for a panel that arrives; the overshoot stays small and applies to arrivals and confirmations, not to every movement. Reduce motion replaces it by a fade.
- **Same slide-in for the Project panel** (owner): the project settings pane arrives like the Look pane, with the same bounce; keep it identical.
- **Forms (owner, after DS1): "the input fields are too narrow and not elegant."** For DS3: every text field fills the width of its pane (a flexible width with a sensible minimum, never a fixed narrow box), one control height and one corner radius from the tokens, a visible label above or beside it in a fixed column, a placeholder that is a real example, a clear focus ring in the accent, multi-line fields that grow with their text, chips for lists such as languages, inline validation next to the field. Check whether the pane itself is too narrow: a resizable pane with a remembered width, and a wider default, may matter more than the field style.
- **Clap in the rail (owner, after DS1).** Ask Clap becomes an entry of the left rail, next to Project and Build, instead of a tab of the right panel. Proposed shape: two groups in the rail, separated by a thin line. Centre views (Film, Build) and right panels (Inspector, Look, Project, Clap). A centre view and a right panel are independent (this also removes the DS1 oddity where choosing Film or Build closed the settings pane). A right panel slides in with the same spring as Look and Project. The Clap entry carries its state: a pulse while Clap works, a badge with the number of proposals waiting for the owner, and the Memory, Sessions and Journal tabs stay inside the Clap panel. A shortcut focuses the Clap input from anywhere. Do before DS2 and DS3 so that they restyle the final structure.
- **Conversation language (owner, after DS1).** The setting is redundant because Clap answers in the language the owner writes. Default it from the Mac's preferred language (English if unsupported), prefilled at creation, editable in a corner of the settings, no longer a required question (the base configuration count drops from 5 to 4 and the interview gate follows); the instruction to Clap: reply in the language of the user, use the stored language for the first message and when unsure; the film's on-screen languages stay a real question. Planned as CL1, before DS3.
- **Shell geometry and the word Film (owner, after DS1).** The top bar stays as it is. The rail, the left panels (Storyboard on top, Resources below) and the right panel take the full height of the window; the centre column holds the preview and, under it, the strip, which is now only as wide as the centre column. The strip is called **Film** (the timeline of the film, under its preview); the left list is the **Storyboard** (the plan of the scenes and transitions). This replaces the earlier vocabulary note that gave the strip the name Storyboard. Built as part of RL1; to check: the strip at its narrower width (Fit, zoom, ruler, grid, diamonds) and the smallest window size.
- **Film placement toggle (owner).** One button in a view group at the top right of the top bar, whose icon is a pictogram of the layout in use (the icon represents the arrangement chosen), chooses where the Film strip lives: under the preview (default, the centre column's width, with the rail, left panels and right panel at full height) or full width (a block along the bottom from the left panel to the right panel; the rail stays full height). Remembered per project, kept by the reset icon (which restores the sizes only, never the placement), animated with the panel spring. Built in RL1.
- **Sizes and reset (owner).** The widths and heights the owner drags (left panels width, right panel width, Film height, the Storyboard and Resources divider) are the owner's own values and are never changed by switching the Film placement; a window too small only clamps them for drawing. A reset icon next to the layout button restores the standard proportions and keeps the chosen placement; it replaces the old "Reset layout" control. Built in RL1.

**Built in DS2 (2026-11-10).** The Film strip and the Storyboard list read the design tokens (no colour literal left in either file). The strip's pictures are clipped to their frame and cropped from the left; frames at least 104 pt high have a header band (label, Status icon) and a footer band (duration). Motion: `MotionTokens` (ShowreelKit) and `Motion.swift` (modifiers: nudge, state pulse, toggle icon, arrive, depart, settle, pop, run mark); the three panes use the panel spring token; a source guard forbids raw durations and curves in views. Left for DS3: the inspector and the forms (148 colour picks in 30 files). Not done: the selected highlight slides between rows and frames (it fades today); the drop-target highlight of a diamond keeps its existing look.
- **Alignment in the inspector (owner, screenshot of the State section, after RL1).** In the rows Visible, Ready and Locked the icons have different widths, so the three labels start at different x positions and the column looks ragged. For DS3: every row that pairs an icon with a label uses one shared row layout with a fixed-width icon slot (so labels align on one left edge) and a fixed trailing slot for its switch; the same rule for every icon and label row of the app (lists, menus, forms). The Instruction field is greyed in that state: check whether that is its disabled look and that it says why.
- **Real tabs (owner, screenshot of the scene inspector).** "Settings, Composition, Effects, Look" must look like tabs, not like a segmented control. Rule for DS3: tabs switch panes of content and use the underline style (the active label in primary text with an accent underline, inactive labels in secondary text, a hairline under the bar, a hover state); segmented controls stay for choosing a mode or a value (such as Library or Media). Apply the tab style to the scene inspector, the Project and Look tabs of the settings pane, and the Conversation, Memory, Sessions and Journal tabs of the Clap panel. The underline moves between tabs with the base duration (no slide under "Reduce motion"), the tabs are keyboard reachable (arrows) and announced as tabs.
- **Other details seen on the same screenshot (not verified in the code), for DS3:** "Delete scene..." uses the default grey button and should use the destructive style; "Duplicate" sits outside the State card and looks detached; a two-line text field shows a scroll track at its right edge even though nothing scrolls (use overlay scrollbars shown only when needed); a tooltip covered the Duration label (check the tooltip delay and where a pane-level help is attached, so a tooltip about the composition does not appear over the Duration field).
- **Duration stepper (owner).** Numeric fields such as a scene's Duration get small up and down chevrons at the right end of the field (arrow keys too; Option for fine steps, Shift for coarse), with limits, rounding and a reason when disabled. Added to DS3.
- **Duplicate removed (owner: "I did not ask for it").** The Duplicate button leaves the interface everywhere (inspector, menus, list, strip); this replaces the earlier note that suggested moving it into the State card. The typed operation and any Clap tool that uses it stay until the owner decides. Done in DS3.
- **State section: no Instruction field (owner, "this mechanic makes no sense").** The two-step "switch Ready off, write the instruction, press Return" is removed. The State section is three switches and Delete scene. A change is asked for by editing the scene's own fields (saving sends the scene back to To review) or by talking to Clap with the scene selected. Old notes stay stored and show read-only. Done in DS3. This settles the open question "Switch Ready off?" in docs/ideas.md.
- **No Save, no Revert (owner: "what you type or add is saved, period").** The editing forms autosave: text on blur, Return or a short pause; numbers on blur, Return or after a step (debounced); switches and choices at once; an invalid value never commits and keeps its draft with an inline error. One undo step per edit session; a small "Saved" mark and, when the scene goes back to To review, one line in the status line with Undo. Transactional flows (create a project, Build and Generate cards, Clap proposals, package save and import, the rights question, Delete confirmation) stay explicit. Planned as AS1, before DS3.

- 2026-11-10 (owner, after AS1/DS3 prompts): the scene inspector's Effects tab has no value (only the per-item animation choice and a read-only pack line). Remove it; the animation choice moves into Composition, on each item's row. Built in DS3 (section 2b).

**Built in AS1 (2026-11-10).** No Save or Revert in the scene inspector, the palette editor, a content item and a transition: `EditSession` (ShowreelKit) decides when a field commits (text: blur, Return or a 0.7 s pause; numeric: blur, Return or a 0.3 s step debounce; instant: at once; an invalid draft never commits), one undo step per field per 2 s (`coalesceLast`), a "Saved" and an "Updated" mark, the To review line once per session. Left: the project form, the other Look editors (fonts, background, mood), the file editor and the resource details still have their own commit controls.

**Built in DS3 (2026-11-10).** `FormLayout`, `NumericField` and `TabBarModel` (ShowreelKit), `FormViews.swift` (icon rows, tab bar, stepper field, chip, Project button); the Effects tab and the scene Duplicate and Instruction field removed; wider panes; the inspector, forms, Clap panel and Settings on the tokens. Left: one text-field style for every field, multi-line fields that grow without an empty scroll track, a review of every button's role, the tooltip placement review, and the 109 colour picks in other files (DS4 or owner decisions).

**Built in DS3b (2026-11-10).** `ThemedTextFieldStyle` (fill, `FormLayout` height and radius, accent ring, `.fieldError`, dimmed), `GrowingTextEditor` (the `FormLayout.areaLines` rule, scroll bar only past the limit), `StepperKeys` (a local key monitor on macOS 13, `NumericField.keyStep`), roles on the standalone buttons. Left native: the Settings window and the package and new-project sheets (native `Form` rows), the Clap composer, the file editor's text view, and the buttons inside menus, alerts and context menus.
