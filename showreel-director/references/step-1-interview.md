# Step 1 detail: interview, express mode, assets, brief

Full rules of Step 1. The question bank and brief template are in `interview.md`.

Everything the build depends on is decided here: palette, energy, music, claims, formats **and the assets the animation will be made from**. Read `references/interview.md` (question bank). Unless the user already said to go fast, ask one question on depth: **full interview** (recommended for a first reel; **express** is recommended instead when a detailed brief file was supplied or the user said to go fast, and it is always confirmed by this question unless the user explicitly asked for it), **I propose, you correct** or **express** (below). **I propose, you correct:** draft rounds 1 to 4 yourself from the material and the brief, run the feasibility check on the draft (it replaces the checks after rounds 1 and 2b), and present one proposal sheet that doubles as the brief review (one card per decision area, Keep / Change with a required note); a card the user keeps is a *decision*, an item not on the sheet is an *assumption* and is listed as such in the brief; the effects lab and round 5 still run. Use the tools of the interaction rule, in this order:

1. **Mission & message** — purpose, audience, where it will be played, target duration (and any cut-downs wanted), source material, the one-sentence message, and the attributes the brand should be perceived as (easy, simple, fun, robust, modern, cool, premium, human…) with what will show each rather than say it.
2. **Story & voice** — arc, tone/persona, claims policy and confidentiality, closing line/CTA.
2b. **Emotion & soul** — the feeling the film must create (motivating, engaging, impactful, reassuring, convincing, inspiring…), what it must not feel like, the viewer's shift from → to, the tension shape and where it peaks, and the human anchor or memorable moment. This drives pace, energy, music, colour and silence, so settle it before the look.
3. **Look & motion** — references, visual language, palette/typography, devices, energy curve, WOW moment, effects. Don't ask the user to describe effects: let them see them. Once palette and font are known, build the effects lab with `python3 scripts/build_effects_lab.py --out <project>/qa/lab-effects.html` (pass the brand colours and font), present it, and ask them to choose one or two moods first (the page opens on the six packs of `references/effects-catalogue.md`, never on all 248 effects), then to click the hearts on the effects of those moods and paste "Copy my picks" back in the chat (the picks start with the chosen moods). The full list stays one click away (`--full` opens on it). Then offer per-category choices by name, filtered by the brand rules and the picked moods; **typography:** build the font picker (`python3 scripts/build_font_picker.py --out <project>/qa/fonts-picker.html`, brand colours, `--ui`/`--lang`) so the user picks one free font for titles and one for text ("Copy my picks" returns "Titles: X | Text: Y"), unless a brand font is supplied; fetch the chosen files with `scripts/fetch_font.py "<name>"` (or ask the user to download them from the specimen link if the network is blocked), hand the folder to the organiser, and check glyph coverage for every reel language; if the lab cannot be opened, fall back to the named menu with one-line descriptions.
4. **Sound, accessibility & delivery** — track, sound design, narration, aspect ratios, player controls (multi-select checklist), what the tools can actually deliver (MP4?), hosting/sharing, deadline and feedback rounds.
5. **Asset inventory** — logos, brand rules, fonts, screenshots, footage, photos, icons, figures, tracks, narration, references, legal. For each class: provided / to fetch / to build procedurally / not needed.

**Feasibility gate (be a critical director).** Right after round 1, again after round 2b and again after the storyboard, check duration, scene count, amount of text, languages, formats and track against `references/feasibility.md` and `scripts/feasibility.py`. Don't ask the user for a scene count: derive a structure (sections, seconds, scenes, words), start `brief.md` right after round 1 with a Feasibility section and append to it as the interview goes (formats are asked in round 4, so re-run the check then: portrait budgets are tighter), and when there are real alternatives let the user choose between two or three structures on a decision sheet. If the user states counts or supplies a storyboard, check them at once. When something cannot work (for example 30 shots in 10 seconds, or 100 words of text in 3 languages in 10 seconds), say so with the numbers, name the standard or finding behind the limit, and offer two or three workable options with the recommended one first; never silently comply or silently shrink the request. Record the decision in the brief. Standards are for orientation: when exact platform specifications matter (maximum length, safe zones, loudness), search the platform's current documentation.

**Express mode (the one definition; `interview.md` points here).** If the user supplies a detailed brief or says to go fast, replace rounds 1–4 (including 2b) with one confirmation round: in the first reply, before the round 0 questions (when express is chosen only later through the depth question, in the reply that follows its answer), restate what you understood in at most five lines, run the feasibility check on it and add one line naming the gates that still follow (asset audit, brief and style sheet validation, storyboard, scene review, look test, release gate); round 0 is the first thing you ask, alone in its question block; once it is answered, ask, on a decision sheet or through the question tool, one question per assumption to confirm and one per missing decision, covering at least: the intended feeling and the viewer's shift, the tension peak, music, formats and controls, accessibility defaults, claims policy. Effects are not asked by name: present the sample lab and let the user heart what they like (or record the recommended pack as an assumption). The express sheet doubles as the brief review: do not validate the same points a second time on a brief sheet, only what the sheet did not cover (derived structure, assets). Whatever stays unanswered is filled with the recommended option **and recorded as an assumption**, so no brief section is empty. Round 0, the asset audit of round 5, the storyboard (Step 2), the scene-by-scene review (Step 2b), the look test and every gate above are never skipped: express shortens the interview only.

Rules for the interview:
- **Vocabulary.** Users may not come from video or editing and may use everyday or imprecise words ("slide" or "clip" for a scene, "effect", "zoom", "punchy"). Before the interview, skim `references/glossary.md`: section 1 maps everyday phrases to what they usually mean and what to settle, section 2 lists words that mean two things, section 3 lists easily confused pairs. Interpret the intent, keep the user's words in conversation, and use the precise term in the brief and storyboard with the user's word in brackets the first time ("scene (your slide)"). In the first reply, say in one line how each vague word will be settled ("punchy" and "vibe": we will pick pace and effects by looking at them; "zoom": which kind of camera or transition move). Restate once, kindly and briefly, when a term's precise meaning would change the work (a one-take is not a scene); when two readings lead to different work, ask one short question naming both. Never correct tone or imply the user is wrong. When asked what a term means, answer in one or two lines with an example from their own project, and point to the glossary for the rest.
- **Cut-downs.** If a short and a long version were asked for in round 1, record each cut with its duration in the brief; the storyboard marks which scenes belong to each cut, and Step 4 builds each cut as its own schedule.
- Skip what the conversation, an uploaded file or a URL already answers, but restate it in one line so the user can correct it. Pre-fill recommended options from the source (brand site, deck, brief) rather than asking blind.
- Look at the assets before asking about them: read the brief and list the asset folder first, so questions can name real files and their real problems. When problems are already visible (file named in the message, folder listing, `audit_assets.py` flags), name each one in your first reply in one line with the default you propose (for example "logo is 96x24: use the vector version", "font forbids embedding: closest free font, said aloud", "video too heavy: stills or frame sequence", "photo without confirmed rights: excluded"); the decision itself is taken in round 5.
- **Recommendations must respect earlier answers.** Check every recommended option against what the user already chose (don't recommend a loop after "plays once"). If an answer changes a previous decision, say so in the question. Derive recommended options from the brief and the brand (tone, charter), not from habit.
- Round 5 is not optional. Ask for the files (chat or connected folder), check `uploads/` and the connected folder yourself, then audit the originals with `scripts/audit_assets.py` and by eye: resolution, transparency, licence (music, fonts, photos), weight, personal data, image rights. Proposed files become `approved` when the user confirms the assets item of the brief sheet (then `--set id=approved`); nothing is approved silently. State your default for every flagged asset (for example "excluded unless you confirm the image rights") so the user only has to correct, not decide from scratch. Then put everything in its place with `scripts/organize_assets.py` (plan first, show held files on a decision sheet, then `--apply`; originals go to `assets/source/`, embeddable copies get typed names in `assets/prepared/`, statuses are recorded with `--set`, the manifest table for the brief comes from `--md`; conventions in `references/assets.md`). Sensitive content in a screenshot (customer data, names, tokens) is removed from the pixels of a copy and added with `--redacted` (paint over or replace it, then check the result); the original never reaches `prepared/`; a blur or mask applied at render time is not enough, because the original image would still sit inside the delivered file. Anything missing gets an explicit fallback (procedural drawing, placeholder flagged on screen, or "user to supply by <date>"). Don't copy personal data from an excluded file into the brief.
- **Beat map as soon as there is a track.** When the track file is in the folder (round 4 or 5), run `scripts/analyze_audio.py` straight away, then confirm tempo, first beat and the proposed moments (drop, breakdown return, ending) with the user on a decision sheet or by question; record them in the brief. Storyboard times are bars from this map. Without a track, times follow the reading budget.
- Open questions that multiple choice can't capture (exact claim wording, names that must not appear, the track's title) go in one plain-text question at the end of the round.
- Don't start production before the answers; if the user says "you choose", pick the recommended option and record it as an assumption.

**Consistency review (before writing the brief).** Re-read all answers once and fix contradictions yourself or ask one short question (checklist in `references/interview.md`, section "Consistency review").

Then write `brief.md` in the project folder, in the conversation language (template in `references/interview.md`): every decision, every assumption, the **asset manifest** (file, role, scene, status, licence, weight) and a **release gate** listing every placeholder and unvalidated item that must be resolved before the reel leaves the user's hands. The brief is the source of truth for every later step and feedback round; update it instead of relying on memory. Present the file, then validate it with a decision sheet (one item per section that carries a decision: message and audience, feeling and look, sound, delivery and assets, derived structure; options Keep / Change with a required note). The asset manifest's scene column and the structure table are completed in Step 2b, when scenes exist. Never paste the brief in the chat.
